# Review skill: experiment and evaluation

The goal is a review that reports every problem in a change, behavior errors, unnecessary mechanisms, unclear boundaries and tests that would miss a regression, and what it could not explain, each grounded in the code, from a model of Sonnet's class. The skill supports one reviewer or independent reviewers by direction.

## Rounds 1 to 10: one private change

A first experiment used one non-public change as a fixed target, with a fresh reviewer per round and no execution. Ten reviews were run while the instructions changed; independent reviewers assessed results and earlier instructions were retained. The per-round classification is in [rounds.md](rounds.md). This is formative evidence, not a controlled before/after result: the target, model family, and evaluator were not varied, and only one review was run for most revisions. The original task, code, logs, and identifying details are not included.

[fixtures](fixtures/) holds the two single-file fictional cases prepared in that experiment; one review read the positive case ([rounds.md](rounds.md#fictional-fixture-pilot)). They stay as that run's input. Later rounds use the cases of the instrument below.

## Round 11: a procedure and an instrument

The table of rounds 1 to 10 cannot say which wording helped. Drafts 06 and 07 are one file, as are 08 and 09, and each pair's two reviews were classified differently; one review per draft does not separate a draft's effect from a run's. Round 11 therefore adds the means to measure before changing more wording.

- [The instrument](../../evals/review/README.md) lives with the plugin's other evals: three fictional repositories in two states, rubrics with traps, a proof that makes each fact a rubric relies on happen, a script that prepares a blind run and one that scores a judge's verdicts. `evals/review-eval.test.mjs` checks it without a model.
- [drafts/11-procedure.md](drafts/11-procedure.md) is the page as committed: the same four directions as a numbered procedure that collects candidates in separate passes, refutes each one, and reports in a fixed shape with a place for what stayed unexplained. It is invoked by the user only, and orchestrate's cross-reviewer follows it. The draft's author also wrote the cases, the same day.

A pilot on Sonnet and a round on Terra followed ([rounds.md](rounds.md#round-11-terra-round)). On Terra, either draft found what no instructions missed, an unreachable catch, an unused option and a rule implemented twice; drafts 10 and 11 differed by one item in thirty-two while draft 11's reviews were 1.8 times as long; no review stepped into a trap. On the private change, draft 11 established no dependency contract and proposed no decomposition, as draft 10 had not.

## Round 12: a target, and cases that have one

The round's three cases were solved by every arm, so they are retired, and the instrument now holds cases written for what the skill is for: a change that works badly because it is built badly. Each has the change as it was first written and as it should be, and both are reviewed ([the instrument](../../evals/review/README.md)): one function doing five jobs against a plan in three short ones, a framework of nine files against one step function, guards read off a stale contract against one conversion at the source. The checks that state each brief pass on the clean form and fail on the other where its rubric says.

[drafts/12-target.md](drafts/12-target.md), the page as committed, changes draft 11 in four places, each a hypothesis the next round tests:

- a step that sketches the smallest clean solution before findings are filtered, and a line for it in the verdict, so that findings add up to a direction;
- a design pass that judges structure in both directions, too little and too much, names the crutch as its own kind, and leaves one line per changed unit in the report, so that the examination can be seen;
- draft 10's rule for a dependency, its version from the lockfile and the installed package rather than a document or another copy, and its counterpart for stored data, every writer;
- a report of half the fields: a finding is its place, what happens and the fix.

[drafts/13-blocks.md](drafts/13-blocks.md) adds one step to draft 12 before any review was run with it, and was the page as committed until draft 15: the reviewer first splits the diff into blocks, the smallest groups of lines that make one decision, says of each what it does and which requirement it serves, and reports that map, one line per block. Necessity then is asked of every line, not only of the mechanisms a reviewer happens to notice.

[drafts/17-screens.md](drafts/17-screens.md) is the page as committed: [draft 16](drafts/16-ideas.md) plus one part of the behavior pass and one section of the report for a change the user sees. The reviewer works from the pictures it was given, the mockup, the screen before and the screen after, gives each difference from the mockup its reason or makes it a finding, and does not start the application unless told to. No review has been run with that part: no case has pictures. Draft 16 is [draft 15](drafts/15-structure.md) plus a recommendation under the verdict, what the reviewer would do next and in what order, and a section of ideas, improvements the change does not need, each with its gain and cost, kept apart from the findings. Draft 15 adds to draft 13 a summary of the change at the head of the report (what it is for, why, its parts, the sources), the units of the change with what each uses, as written and for the target, a "Dependencies" part of the design pass over them, and a pointer to the interactive report: the same report written as data, which a script in the skill checks against the diff and draws as a page. [report-trial/](report-trial/README.md) records both passes of that. No judged round has run with draft 15, 16 or 17.

That round ran on Terra ([rounds.md](rounds.md#round-17-terra-round-2)). On the first-written forms either draft found nineteen items of twenty against fourteen with no instructions, and drafts 13 and 17 did not differ, at a fifth more words for draft 17. On a clean form both drafts turned a race nobody triggers into a finding and a lock into the target; one clean fixture turned out to hold a real defect; on the private change the installed dependency's contract was again not established and the interleaved rules again not separated. The interactive report rendered for every review and showed three misleading states. The page was corrected for those states and reordered as the user asked ([report-trial](report-trial/README.md#fourth-pass-the-page-after-the-user-read-one)); the skill's page was not changed. [references.md](references.md) is a reading of what is known about how reviews find things, done before the next draft so that it adds methods and not one more rule for the fault a round showed.

[drafts/18-methods.md](drafts/18-methods.md) is the page as committed. It adds no rule for the race of round 17. Step 2 now writes two lists: the entries, what starts the changed code, and the contracts, for each boundary which side guarantees what and the line of the running version that says so. A defect's trigger must be one of the entries, and every finding carries a "Shown by" line; one with nothing to put there is a question. The design pass takes each requirement as the one that changes: requirements that can change apart and alternate inside one unit are a reason to split, one requirement spread over several units a reason to merge, and size is neither. A patch for a library the project cannot change stays, with the fix in the library as an idea. The report gives each requirement its source and state, the recommendation as steps, and speaks the user's language; the data of the interactive report follows. The page is 2810 words against 2470.

The planned comparison of `control`, draft 17 and draft 18 over the three cases in both forms, and one private-change review with draft 18, is recorded in [Terra round 3](terra-round-3/README.md). Its rows are not directly comparable with the previous round because the cases were corrected between them. The record separates the original rubric judgments from later disputes about the supported contracts.

## Design calibration

The [follow-up calibration](terra-design-calibration/README.md) adds a versioned import case with explicit design criteria, a behavior-correct booking case, a good control and an independent implementation stage. It compares draft 18 with no skill, with two reviews per cell and six implementers. Both arms found the main booking design issues, and a task-only implementer produced the same production code as the draft 18 review-guided sample. This is limited evidence from small synthetic cases, not proof of reliability or a measure of overall code quality.

## Draft 19

Two reviews of the private change with draft 18 took out the extra lookup and left the rules of its validator in one piece, and the calibration's judges found two faults in the page itself, both confirmed in its text: the design pass judged "against the target of step 5", a step that comes after it, and "interleaved" meant blocks that alternate, which rules written one after another in one function do not. There is a third. The test of draft 18 took each requirement as the one that changes, and the rules that need separating sit inside one requirement, so the test had nothing to find.

[drafts/19-rules.md](drafts/19-rules.md) is the page as committed, and no review has been run with it. For a unit that decides, the map of step 3 now holds its rules, written from the code: what each reads, when it applies, what comes out and which wins. The design pass takes each rule, and each requirement, as the one that changes, and counts the lines that change makes one read; rules that cannot be read apart are tangled whether or not their blocks alternate, and rules written in the order they win, each reading its own inputs, are apart already in one function or in five. The pass no longer refers to the target; step 5 builds the target from the passes and walks every requirement and rule through it. A design finding needs that count before and after, and a structure that only reads better is an idea: one of two draft 18 reviews of a clean import made a merge a mandatory fix. A removal says what an observer sees change with no further input, after time passes or on a retry, and one that rests on a dependency needs the line of the installed version or it is a question; a mechanism that goes takes its request, state, timer and tests with it. The report gives the verdict as a sentence and the lines that decide it with no count of findings, then how the change works now, the target and the recommendations; a requirement's text no longer carries what was not checked; `traced` is `read`; scenarios to check by hand have a section, written only from what the reviewer read and marked as not run; and the change map lists the blocks to fix or remove a line each and the kept ones several to a line. The page is 3315 words against 2810.

The interactive report follows the same shape ([report-trial](report-trial/README.md#fifth-pass-after-the-user-read-a-report-on-a-real-change)). Two faults of the page were found by the calibration and confirmed in its script: a note on a finding left undecided, and on an idea not taken, was dropped from the text the user hands back.

## The instrument after the calibration

The calibration showed what the rubric could not: a review can name every listed item and still not lead to the simpler solution, and a case the unaided reviewer solves tells the arms apart by nothing. [The instrument](../../evals/review/README.md) now has a second stage that measures the code left when someone acts on a review. `implement.mjs` gives each review to a fresh implementer, adds implementers that see only the task, and `outcome.mjs` runs the case's acceptance checks on every result, counts what was changed and how much code is left against the clean form, and reads a judge's rulings on the case's probes: later changes, each a local edit in the clean form. The judge rules on the case's own two forms without knowing it, and is not counted where it cannot tell them apart. On a clean form the lines an implementer changed are the measure of a review's false findings, with no judge. A case counts toward the result only if the unaided arms fail on it.

Two cases were written for the kinds of [references.md](references.md#kinds-of-finding-the-instrument-does-not-cover) and for what the private change showed twice. `profile-phone` has a contract read off a stale page, a decision resting on a false claim, a sibling path left unchanged and a forced patch for a library. `slot-edit` is the class of the private change: a form whose five rules are written one after another and tied by one overwritten variable, behind a lookup of what the input already carries, with a cache, a retry and loading state for it, and a timer that looks removable and is not, because the installed form library does not validate on submit. Neither has been run: whether the unaided arms fail on them is the first thing the next round shows.

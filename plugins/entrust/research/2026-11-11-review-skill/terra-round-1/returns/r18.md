## Findings

- **[P1] Cart prices are permanently reduced on every summary update.** [total.js:14](case/head/src/checkout/total.js:14) changes `item.price` in place. `renderSummary` runs again with the same cart on quantity changes or coupon application and displays those prices ([summary.js:3](case/head/src/checkout/summary.js:3)). A $100 item becomes $95 on the first calculation and $90.25 on the next; adding a coupon also starts from the already reduced price. Calculate the discount from the subtotal without modifying cart items.

- **[P1] Free shipping uses the discounted subtotal for loyal customers without a coupon.** [total.js:14](case/head/src/checkout/total.js:14) reduces prices before the subtotal is calculated, and [line 32](case/head/src/checkout/total.js:32) uses that reduced subtotal for shipping. A qualifying $50 cart is charged shipping even though the task requires the pre-discount subtotal to determine eligibility. The returned subtotal is also $47.50 rather than $50. Preserve the original subtotal for both purposes.

- **[P1] The cache can retain obsolete loyalty and staff status.** [loyalty.js:5](case/head/src/checkout/loyalty.js:5) caches the first result indefinitely by customer ID. If that customer later completes another order or becomes staff, subsequent calculations still use the old result; in the latter case a previously loyal customer can receive both discounts. Evaluate eligibility from the supplied customer each time. The eligibility check is inexpensive, and no invalidation mechanism is present.

- **[P2] Exactly five completed orders do not qualify.** [loyalty.js:6](case/head/src/checkout/loyalty.js:6) uses `>` even though the requirement says five or more. Use `>=` and cover the boundary.

- **[P2] Per-item rounding changes the discount.** [total.js:15](case/head/src/checkout/total.js:15) rounds each unit price before multiplying by quantity, whereas the requirement applies 5% to the subtotal. For 100 items at $0.01, each discounted unit rounds back to $0.01, so the customer receives no discount instead of $0.05. Apply the rate to the aggregate after the coupon, using the existing total and tax rounding rules.

- **[P2] The new option can bypass a required discount.** [total.js:9](case/head/src/checkout/total.js:9) adds `skipLoyalty`, although the task requires the discount for qualifying non-staff customers and no repository caller uses the option. Remove the option unless there is an independently specified checkout flow that requires an override.

## Design and tests

`computeTotal` now calculates item subtotals, applies loyalty either while iterating items or after the coupon, applies staff discount and clamps the result, then calculates shipping and tax. The item loop also mutates input. The two loyalty paths make it harder to verify that coupon and non-coupon orders use the same subtotal rule. A single loyalty step after the optional coupon would keep that rule together and remove the mutation and per-item rounding.

`isLoyal` combines the staff exclusion and order threshold with a process-wide cache. The eligibility rules belong together, but the cache introduces an unrelated lifetime and invalidation concern with no demonstrated need.

The new tests cover a high-order-count customer and a staff customer, but the loyal-customer test expects the reduced subtotal and therefore endorses the shipping defect. Add coverage for four versus five orders, a coupon followed by loyalty, the $50 shipping boundary, repeated summary updates with the same cart, changing customer eligibility, and a quantity where per-unit rounding differs from subtotal discounting.

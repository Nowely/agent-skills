## Findings

1. **[P1] Repeated summary renders repeatedly discount and mutate the cart.** [total.js:13](case/head/src/checkout/total.js:13) changes `item.price` in place for a loyal customer without a coupon. [summary.js:3](case/head/src/checkout/summary.js:3) explicitly says it reruns with the same cart after updates: a $100 item becomes $95, then $90.25, and its displayed unit price changes too. Applying a coupon afterward uses the already-discounted price. Calculate the discount from an unchanged subtotal instead.

2. **[P1] Free shipping and subtotal use discounted prices without a coupon.** [total.js:14](case/head/src/checkout/total.js:14) discounts each item before building `subtotal`, which then controls shipping at line 32. A qualifying customer with a $50 cart is charged $4.90 shipping because the subtotal becomes $47.50, contrary to the explicit pre-discount threshold. The returned subtotal also becomes $47.50. Per-item rounding can additionally change the discount: ten $0.05 items receive no discount when each unit is rounded separately. Build the original subtotal first and apply 5% to the discounted amount after the coupon.

3. **[P1] Cached eligibility becomes stale as customer data changes.** [loyalty.js:5](case/head/src/checkout/loyalty.js:5) caches the first result indefinitely by ID. A customer checked with four completed orders remains ineligible after their order count reaches six; a cached non-staff result can also continue granting loyalty after that customer becomes staff. Eligibility is a simple check against the current customer fields, so calculate it on each call.

4. **[P2] Exactly five completed orders do not qualify.** [loyalty.js:6](case/head/src/checkout/loyalty.js:6) uses `>` rather than `>=`, although the requirement says five or more. Use an inclusive comparison.

## Simplicity and structure

`skipLoyalty` adds a public way to bypass a required discount, but the task gives no such exception and the repository contains no caller using it. Removing the option preserves the requested behavior. The new `loyaltyApplied` output also has no consumer in the repository or stated requirement; its removal would change the returned object, so any external consumer would need checking first.

In `computeTotal`, the individual rules are subtotal accumulation, loyalty eligibility, coupon subtraction, loyalty discount, staff discount, clamping, shipping, and tax. Loyalty is currently split between the item loop and coupon branch, with cart mutation interleaved with subtotal calculation. That makes a change to discount order or rounding require tracing both paths and their side effects. A single flow—original subtotal, coupon, applicable discount, clamp, shipping and tax—keeps those rules together while preserving the specified order. In `isLoyal`, staff exclusion and the order threshold are cohesive checks; the cache introduces a separate lifetime rule that the requirement does not need.

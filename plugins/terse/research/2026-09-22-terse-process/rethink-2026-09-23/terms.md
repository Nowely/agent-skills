## How to read the table

- **Who parses it** is a prediction about readers, level 2 at best; the tests under *The five decisions I am least sure of*
  would make five of them level 3. Three readers from the profile (`audit.md:29-33`): **Dev**, who owns a project's
  docs and writes code; **Writer**, who is not an engineer ("NOT NECESSARILY AN ENGINEER"); **RU**, who
  reads Russian and English and may meet an English idiom as a second-language reader. A sense not backed by a count below is
  general usage, not fetched.
- **n/39** is the number of the 39 documents fetched in stage 1 whose text contains the word (word-bounded,
  case-insensitive grep); **h n/39** counts their headings. Commands and output: `terms-evidence/`
  (`count-genre.sh` → `genre.txt`, `count-headings.sh` → `headings.txt`; `run.sh` regenerates all three). `[KEY]:N` is line N of that
  document's saved copy in `survey/synthesis-sources/` or `survey/fetched/`, keys as in the synthesis's
  *Fetched URL key*. G = the 9 exact-genre plugin READMEs.
- **Cost outside** is what a README rename leaves or requires outside `README.md`. No rename below
  *requires* an edit elsewhere: a term the reference files use is for readers who are agents
  (`stages.md:295-298`, rule 8). What is listed is where a person still meets the old word: **M** = the
  description string in `plugins/terse/.claude-plugin/plugin.json:4` and `.claude-plugin/marketplace.json:20`
  (the plugin listing), each skill's frontmatter `description` (the skill picker), and what a skill prints
  to the user. Agent-facing counts are lines outside `README.md` under `plugins/terse/` plus the marketplace
  file (`count-pages.sh` → `pages.txt`); they stay. A rename in the README alone changes no CHANGELOG line; renaming a page would need its own entry.
- **Frames.** Where the README's claim is that the plugin does what a known practice does, the words come
  from that practice: a test (questions, answer key, score, with and without your text), fact-checking
  (claim, false), an editor's rounds (draft, rounds of edits, pass, diff), code review (reviewers). One claim
  the README does not make — that an AI reader is the equal of a person (`owner-readme-words.md:86`) — so
  row 10 keeps the AI in the word.

## The five decisions I am least sure of

1. **"reader" → "AI reader" (row 10).** The pages, the session and the audit's report all say "readers";
   "AI reader" may be parsed as a read-aloud or summarising tool, and "AI" repeated can read as a sales word.
   The cheaper alternative — keep "reader", define it once as an AI agent — is what round 04 did. *Settles
   it:* a reader test on the opening sentence in both forms, one fresh reader per form, asked "Who answers the
   questions in this test — people or software?" and "Does this measure how people read your text?" A form
   that draws "people" on either question fails; between two that pass, the owner's word.
2. **"pipeline" → "workflow" (row 51).** «Пайплайн» is the owner's word, twice in his purpose statement
   (`purpose.md:6, 8`), and the record of his verdict on round 04 speaks of "the pipeline" (`purpose.md:25`); renaming the
   owner's word for his own method needs his word. *Settles it:* ask him. If "pipeline" must stay, keep it for
   the method inside each skill and "workflow" for the three commands. Or a reader test: "After
   `/terse:audit` finishes, what starts next?" — "rewrite starts by itself" fails.
3. **"documentation" → "text" (row 7).** The mission word must be as wide as the owner's intent, but the
   measured runs are all on READMEs, the readers open only `.md` (audit/SKILL.md:91-92), and no page says how a
   non-English text is read (`audit.md:953-964`, the planted question). "Text" can be read as a promise for
   any format and any language. *Settles it:* one audit on a Markdown text that is not software documentation
   and one on a Russian document, run to the end (level 3), or the owner's wording of the scope fact that sits
   beside the mission. Until then: "text" in the mission, "Markdown documents" in the scope fact.
4. **"skeleton" kept and defined (row 38).** No surveyed document uses it (0/39); the writer's word is
   "outline", the owner's «структура». It stays only because the session and the file say "skeleton"
   (rewrite/SKILL.md:103; audit/SKILL.md:168). *Settles it:* the owner's decision on renaming it in the pages
   — 75 lines, the file name, the verdict text. If he renames it there, "outline" everywhere; if not, keep and
   define.
5. **"candidate" → "draft" (row 40).** "Draft" can read as rough, while what is handed over is a reviewed round

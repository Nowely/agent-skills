# Context

**What it is.** dust is a Rust command-line disk-usage viewer: `du` recast as a compact tree of the largest files and directories, with sizes, hierarchy, and proportional bars. A user can install it with the project’s shell installer, Cargo, a listed package manager, or a release binary; then `dust` scans the current directory, while `dust <dir>` or several path arguments choose other roots. Its normal result is a terminal-sized tree; flags can change depth, entry count, filtering, accounting, links, and presentation, or emit JSON.

Inside that product, `dir_walker.rs` is the collection and reduction stage. It applies traversal filters, builds leaf `Node`s in parallel, waits for every descendant before building a directory `Node`, then removes duplicate inode/device pairs and rolls child values into their parents. It resembles a fork/join tree reduction more than a streaming directory iterator: work fans out through one Rayon scope per root and completed subtrees join upward through shared per-directory state.

**Who reads these comments.** The reader maintains dust or is about to change its directory walker. They already read Rust and recognize `Arc`, `Mutex`, atomics, and Rayon; they do not yet carry the local completion protocol in their head. They arrive beside a dense concurrency change, a retry bug, or a performance edit and need to know which ordering and ownership choices are load-bearing before touching them.

**What they need first.** One invariant unlocks the file: a `PendingDir` starts with a count for its own scan, gains one count for each spawned child directory, and is finalized by the task that completes the last obligation. The synthetic parent makes the root obey the same rule. After the scope joins, a separate pass decides duplicate-inode survivors and computes aggregate values. The comments should distinguish this flat discovery/completion machinery from that later recursive post-pass.

**What makes the comments worth wanting.** They should let a maintainer answer four questions at the edit point: why mutation waits until a complete `read_dir` attempt exists; why the counter increments before spawn; why the counter may use relaxed ordering while child publication uses a mutex; and why a completed child is pushed in the same critical section that decrements its parent. Brief comments on filter scope, deduplication order, and test intent protect behavior that is otherwise easy to infer incorrectly. Comments that only translate a statement into English do not earn space.

**Invisible prerequisites.** The reader needs the project-specific meanings of `Node::size` (allocated/apparent bytes, file count, or a timestamp), `PendingDir::pending`, the outer sentinel, and the two phases of the walk. They do not need an introduction to command lines, Rust syntax, or filesystem traversal. The likely failure point is treating `pending` as a count of children rather than outstanding completions, then moving its increment, decrement, or mutex boundary.

**The one thought.** A directory may be published only after its own listing and every descendant are complete; the unusual state, lock boundaries, and sentinel nodes all exist to preserve that rule without recursive directory discovery.
